N67496 · Vultee BT-13A · built 1942

The Vultee Vibrator.

Not a fighter. A classroom.

Almost every American pilot in the Second World War went through a BT-13, between the Stearman and the AT-6. It shook enough at certain power settings to earn the nickname.

Eighty years on, it is still doing the job it was built for: teaching people to fly, at a flight school, with its training-command markings on the tail. You do not need a licence to ride. If you have one, you can fly it.

Before you book

Anything else? A pilot answers the phone

Is the tour price per person?

No, it is for the airplane. Every scenic tour carries up to three passengers for one price, so the $299 Sombrero Lighthouse tour is about a hundred dollars each split three ways. The Sunset Celebration is a private tour.

Do I need any experience for a discovery flight?

None. The instructor handles takeoff and landing, and once you are clear of the field they hand you the controls. If you keep going, the time is logged toward your Private Pilot certificate.

What if the weather turns on the day?

You do not fly and you are not charged for a flight that did not happen. The pilot makes the call on the day and the tour gets rescheduled. Visiting on a tight window? Book early in your trip.

The airplanes are old. Should that worry me?

No. Every aircraft is maintained and inspected under FAA rules on the same schedule as any other in service. What an older fleet changes is what you learn: steam gauges, stick and rudder, and a paper chart.

Tell us when you are in the Keys.

Dates, who is coming and what you want to fly. You hear back from someone who flies these airplanes.
