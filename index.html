N67496 · Vultee BT-13A · 1942

The Vultee Vibrator.

Not a fighter. A classroom.

Almost every American pilot who flew in the Second World War went through a BT-13. It sat in the middle of the training pipeline, after the Stearman and before the AT-6: the first airplane with real horsepower, a constant-speed prop and a radio, and it It shook enough at certain power settings to earn its nickname.

Which makes it the right airplane for this place. Eighty years on, the BT-13 is still doing the job it was built for, at a flight school, teaching people to fly. Ours is a restored 1942 airframe with its training-command markings still on the tail.

  • Pratt & Whitney R-985 radial
  • Registered N67496, built 1942
  • Training-command markings, tail 21290
  • Pilots and non-pilots welcome

"I believe there's a fountain of youth somewhere in here."

Sol Bradman, owner, on why he keeps teaching. Quoted in Keys Weekly.

Sol Bradman has been flying in the Florida Keys since 1984. He instructed at this academy before he bought it, and has owned and run it since November 2020. The office is upstairs at the EAA Air Museum at Marathon airport, which tells you most of what you need to know about how this place operates.

It is a small operation on purpose. You are taught by the person who owns the airplanes, not assigned to whichever instructor is rotating through on the way to an airline seniority list. There are no deposits and no enrolment fees. You pay as you progress, and if life gets in the way for three months, your money is not sitting in someone else's account.

The other half of what happens here has nothing to do with revenue. The academy runs Young Eagles flights for kids aged 8 to 17, has helped set up an aviation club at Key West High School, and has put Keys teenagers into the left seat who have since gone on to instrument ratings and flight instructor certificates before they finished school.

What that looks like in practice

  • 200+ kids flown free each year through Young Eagles, twice yearly, ages 8 to 17.
  • Aviation club at Key West High School, set up with the academy's help.
  • No deposits. Pay as you progress, not up front.
  • Buzza Foundation scholarships have funded local students training here.
  • Marathon is untowered. You will learn to fit into real traffic on your own radio calls.

Know before you fly.

Prices, ages, what happens on the day, and how flight training in the Keys really works. If yours is not here, call and ask. A pilot answers.

For the airplane. The $299 Sombrero Lighthouse tour, the $399 Key Hopping tour and the $499 Seven Mile Bridge Run all carry up to three passengers for that one price. Split three ways, the twenty minute tour is about a hundred dollars each. The Sunset Celebration at $449 is a private tour.
None at all. A discovery flight is designed for someone who has never touched an airplane. An instructor handles the takeoff and landing, and once you are clear of the field they hand you the controls. If you decide to keep going, that flight time is logged toward your Private Pilot certificate, so it is not money spent twice.
Scenic tours are all ages. For training there is no minimum age to start taking lessons, and plenty of students here begin well before they can drive. The FAA sets the milestones: 16 to fly solo, 17 for a private pilot certificate. Several Keys students have hit those on their birthdays. The academy also flies kids aged 8 to 17 free through the Young Eagles programme twice a year.
Flight time starts at $265 per hour with the aircraft and instructor included, and the honest answer is that the total depends on how often you fly. Students who fly two or three times a week finish in far fewer hours than students who fly once a month, because less of each lesson is spent re-learning the last one. There are no deposits and no enrolment fee, so you pay as you progress. Call with your schedule and you will get a realistic number rather than a brochure figure.
It is a fair question and the answer is no. Age of airframe is not what makes a training aircraft safe. Every aircraft on the line is maintained under FAA rules and inspected on the same schedule as any other aircraft in service, and the Cessna 172 is one of the most proven training designs ever built. What an older fleet does change is what you learn: steam gauges, stick and rudder, and a paper chart, which makes you a better pilot than an autopilot and a moving map do. Come and look at them before you book anything.

The Cessna 310Q is a piston twin, which covers multi-engine time. Most small schools send you to the mainland for both.
Both. You do not need a licence to ride in the 1942 BT-13, and there are multiple mission packages depending on how much of the flying you want to do. If you are a pilot, you can fly it. Pricing depends on the mission, so call and say what you are after.
Flight reviews from $265, instrument proficiency checks, and a rental checkout at $265 per pilot that lets you take an aircraft on your own. Renters insurance is required for that, with minimum coverage of $250k/$25k bodily injury and property damage, $1,000 medical, and $50,000 physical damage to non-owned aircraft. Instructors will also teach in your own aircraft, and there is a Pinch Hitter course covering navigation, radio and landings for whoever normally rides in the right seat.
You do not go, and you are not charged for a flight that did not happen. Keys weather moves fast, especially in summer, and the decision is made by the pilot on the day. A cancelled tour gets rescheduled. If you are visiting on a tight window, book earlier in your trip so there is room to move it.
Keys accommodation is genuinely hard to find, particularly in season, and the academy will help you sort something out for the length of your training rather than leaving you to it. Mention your dates when you get in touch.
