They called it the Vultee Vibrator.

Almost every American pilot in the Second World War learned in a BT-13, between the Stearman and the AT-6. It shook enough at certain power settings to earn the nickname.

Eighty years on, this one still does the job it was built for: teaching people to fly, with its training-command markings on the tail. You do not need a licence to ride. If you have one, you can fly it.

What is under the wing

"I believe there's a fountain of youth somewhere in here."

Sol Bradman, owner, on why he keeps teaching. Quoted in Keys Weekly.

Sol has flown the Florida Keys since 1984. He instructed here before he bought the academy in November 2020, and he still flies with students. You are taught by the person who owns the airplanes, not whoever is rotating through on the way to an airline seat.

Questions before you book

For the airplane. Every scenic tour carries up to three passengers for one price, so the $299 Sombrero Lighthouse tour is about a hundred dollars each split three ways. The Sunset Celebration at $449 is a private tour.
None. The instructor handles takeoff and landing, and once you are clear of the field they hand you the controls. If you keep going, the time is logged toward your Private Pilot certificate, so it is not money spent twice.
Scenic tours are all ages. There is no minimum age to start flight lessons. The FAA sets the milestones: 16 to fly solo, 17 for a Private Pilot certificate. The academy also flies kids aged 8 to 17 free through Young Eagles.
Flight time starts at $265 an hour with the aircraft and instructor included. The total depends on how often you fly: two or three lessons a week finishes in far fewer hours than one a month. No deposits and no enrolment fee. Call with your schedule for a realistic number.
You do not fly and you are not charged for a flight that did not happen. The pilot makes the call on the day and the tour gets rescheduled. Visiting on a tight window? Book early in your trip so there is room to move it.
No. Every aircraft is maintained and inspected under FAA rules on the same schedule as any other in service. What an older fleet changes is what you learn: steam gauges, stick and rudder, and a paper chart. Come and look at them before you book.
